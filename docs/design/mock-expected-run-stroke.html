2 · The decisions this mock is asking for §17.10 — none of these is derivable from the spec

Where a segment ends
The stroke stops. It is not dashed across the gap, and the endpoints are not joined by anything fainter. A dashed line through unprovable time is the same claim in a costume — it says "probably continuous", which is precisely the inference FR-031 removed. The two endpoints keep their point markers, so the eye reads two runs of measurement rather than one interrupted one.
Whether the ruler stays
It stays, and it gains a second band beneath it. They answer different questions and neither replaces the other: the observation ruler is one tick per recorded check — what cerbix SAW — and its empty spans are unobserved time. The expectation ruler is one cell per due window — what cerbix EXPECTED — and its cells carry verdicts. A monitor can have a dense observation ruler and a broken expectation ruler (checks arriving, windows answered late), or the reverse (nothing ran, but the ledger knows it should have). Collapsing them into one band would make those two states look identical.
How a partly-strokable series reads at a glance
From the expectation ruler, in one pass. The stroke above and the solid run of cells below start and stop together, by construction — both come from the same strokeSegments call — so the band is a legend for the line rather than a second opinion about it. Where the band breaks, the line breaks, and the cell that broke it says why.

interval_assumed changes nothing on this page, and that is the point. A covered_late window whose threshold was assumed rather than measured (§14.2) is still covered_late: it breaks the stroke, it is excluded from the coverage numerator, and the flag appears only in the window's readout as confidence. Reading it as licence to promote the window is how this gate would be widened without anyone deciding to widen it — which is why invariant 20g exists, and why the drawing code never consults the flag at all.

4 · Coverage, from the same windows §14.1 — the denominator keeps what the numerator drops

A covered_late window counts in the denominator and not in the numerator. It is not a failure — a run happened and produced an admissible outcome — so dropping it from both would quietly raise the coverage of a monitor that answered its windows from too far away. Panel B's coverage is lower than panel A's for exactly one window, and the stroke it lost is the same window. The two numbers move together because they read the same fact.
