Drawn, and NOT built. The API exposes the current step INDEX and nothing per step; three rows rendered from one integer would be a guess presented as a record — the exact thing this product refuses elsewhere when it withholds a number instead of inventing one. If the list is worth having it needs a read model, and that is its own requirement.
What this mock decides.
  • No new colour. An escalation step is not a new state — it is the existing DOWN story told again to more people — so the fired rows carry the DOWN hue and the pill keeps the degraded hue it already has. Steps not yet due are OUTLINED, never tinted: a step that has not happened is not a state.
  • The policy sits with paging, not on the service form. It answers "who is woken", which is the question that panel exists for. Its own save keeps the two audit actions honest.
  • No renotify field for a service. Monitors have one, services do not, and D8 keeps it that way — the policy's steps are the repeat. A control for a column that does not exist is a promise the backend refuses.
  • No "escalate now", and no pause. The ladder is a function of the incident's start and the policy's offsets; a manual push would be a page nobody can explain from the record afterwards. It already pauses itself when the verdict goes stale or ownership is withdrawn, and acknowledgement is the operator's stop — a third mechanism would give three answers to "why is it quiet".
  • No graph hint. §14's graph annotates and links; it never suppresses, and the ladder does not consult it (D5). A UI implying otherwise would teach the wrong model.
