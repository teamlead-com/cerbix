## 4. What the first attempt got wrong

Recorded because it is the same failure mode this work has been cataloguing, committed while
fixing it.

- **A false causal claim.** "One straggler is enough to keep the older generation in use" —
  the emission gate is EXISTENTIAL: a region moves to generation 3 as soon as one
  credential-ready capability-2 executor exists, and a capability-1 straggler does not hold
  it back. The real consequence is the opposite of a stalled rollout: credentialed work
  concentrates on the capable executors, and if the last one goes away, generation-3 rows
  sit unclaimed until its heartbeat ages out and core falls back. The runbook now says that.
- **A procedure that could not succeed.** It verified drain on `pull_jobs` only, while the
  rollback fence in migration `00063` counts `pull_jobs` AND `pull_tests`, including expired
  rows that survive until the leader's hourly housekeeping. An operator following the
  published steps exactly — jobs clean, queues flat, TTL waited, DLQ handled — would still
  hit the refusal, with nothing in the document to explain it.
- **An instruction to stop something that cannot be stopped.** "Stop the old consumers" is
  not an operator action: the binary consumes every generation it can open. Dropping support
  is a release, and the operator's job is to prove drain before deploying it.
- **Advice that taught ignoring a real fault.** During a correct rotation the old key is
  still in `previous`, so its envelopes open normally and produce no error at all. An
  `unknown_key_id` therefore means the key was removed too early or a payload was left
  undrained — actionable, not "expected traffic".
