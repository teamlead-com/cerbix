## 1. Normative extract (verbatim, gathered BEFORE code)

§11.1: "availability = good_duration / (good_duration + bad_duration)"; "coverage =
(good_duration + bad_duration) / (good_duration + bad_duration + unknown_duration)";
"error budget = (1 − objective) semantics, reusing internal/sla"; "burn rate = multi-window,
reusing the existing rule shapes"; "`excluded_duration` appears in **neither denominator**";
"A zero denominator in either formula yields `unavailable` — **never** `100%` and never `0×`."
The confident-lie case: "129,599 of them entirely `unknown_duration` and one `good`, yields
`100% / 90d` from sixty seconds of measurement" — must be impossible.

§11.2: "Storage continuity: the expected buckets exist, contiguously, through the service's
`sealed_through` watermark"; "Decidable coverage: the fraction above, after exclusion";
"**Both must pass.** Every reliability response carries `as_of`, `sealed_through`, the summed
durations of **both** axes (§9.1), the coverage fraction, and any `repairing` interval. Below
`min_decidable_coverage` (§10.10 [0.95, fixed]) the objective, budget and burn rate are
returned as `partial` **with the fraction and the reason**; at zero decidable time they are
`unavailable`."

§11.3: "The window is therefore `[sealed_through − window, sealed_through)` and the response
says so." "Operators still need a live signal, and it is a **different named thing** — a
categorical `current_health` derived from provisional data, explicitly unstable — not a second
variant of the same percentage. A short burn window lying entirely to the right of
`sealed_through` returns `insufficient_sealed_coverage`, not zero burn and not a number derived
from one bucket." "`sla_targets` gains `service_id` as a third exclusive scope alongside
`monitor_id` / `project_id` (the existing CHECK becomes a three-way exclusivity). Until
phase 5, a service-scoped `burn_alert_enabled` is rejected at both the schema and the
application layer even though the shared table carries the column."

§11.3 (objective): "Phase 2 treats the objective as a **current-view parameter**: the returned
budget always states which objective produced it, and a change is annotated on the timeline."

§12.1: "A window that spans **definition revisions** is returned as **segments**, each with
its revision, its own availability and its own coverage. No aggregate across a definition
boundary is offered — not even labelled"; "A window that spans only **evaluation epochs**
within one definition revision is likewise segmented in the response, but the UI may visually
group adjacent epochs"; "Hour and day rollups are keyed by epoch and never merged across a
boundary of either axis"; "Segments produced by a first-adoption backfill are marked as
declared reconstructions (§6.6), and a `repairing` interval is rendered as such rather than
as data."

§10.2: "Hour and day rollups are **sums of the durations on both axes** over sealed canonical
buckets, **keyed by epoch and never merged across an epoch boundary**. Summation is exact and
associative" — a contract on rollup BEHAVIOR; no stored rollup table is mandated, and this
changeset computes them on read (grouped by epoch, exact integer sums). Stated here so the
choice is reviewable, not implied.

§6.6: "A backfilled segment is therefore a **declared reconstruction** — 'this is what the
window looks like when evaluated with today's members as declared today' — and it is labelled
as such in the API payload and on the timeline." Revision 1 carries "an explicitly retroactive
`effective_at = floor(backfill_start)`" — so the reconstruction interval is derivable:
revision 1 with `effective_at < created_at`, buckets before `CeilToBucket(created_at)`.

§13: "Phase 1–2: service SLOs **calculate = yes, display = yes, alert = no**."

§19 phase-2 invariants 38–47 (all in scope of this changeset except the UI wording of 43's
"grouped visually" and the timeline rendering halves of 44/45, which are the API/UI
changesets' obligations over these payload fields).

  - `ServiceReliabilityReport`: era-anchored (00071 `era_start`), sealed-facts-only window

  - `ServiceReliabilitySeries`: on-read hour/day rollups grouped by (step × epoch × state),

  - `UpsertServiceSLATarget`: objective only — the app layer has NO burn knob to reject.
- **Spec §11.3 amendment + D-0163** own the externally observable choices: fixed 1h/6h burn

## 6. Round 1/2 verdict and the fix pass

Round 1/2 [166]: REJECTED — the three D-0163 interpretations were accepted; three P1s and
three P2s found, all real. Closed as follows (one consolidated pass, round 2/2 requested):

**P1-1 (current_health was sealed history wearing a live name).** In production, provisional
facts are written only by a repair of a range younger than the late-arrival grace — the
forward driver deliberately stops at the horizon — so the old latest-fact read silently fell
back to sealed rows. `ServiceHealthNow` now evaluates the CURRENT canonical bucket ON READ,
through the materializer's own projection (`epochAt` → `observationsFor` [with carry-in] →
`maintenanceSpansFor` → `reliability.Reduce`), and consults NO stored facts at all — a
stalled materializer's sealed HEALTHY history cannot impersonate "now".
`TestServiceHealthNowIsLiveAndRefusesSealedHistory` is product-path only: declaration +
ordinary heartbeat ingest; the sealed history is planted purely as the adversarial
distractor. The series' provisional grouping stays: it is reachable through young-range
repair, stated in the method contract.

**P1-2 (continuity now governs the NUMBERS, not just the status).** The main aggregate and
budget are quotable only when storage continuity holds (status precedence already routes a
gap to partial/storage_gap; the quotable predicate accepts partial ONLY with the
low-coverage reason — and keeps a redundant explicit continuity term as drift defense).
Burn windows now measure their own §11.2 verdicts: `ServiceBurnWindow` carries
expected/sealed buckets, a continuity boolean and the coverage fraction; a gap withholds the
rate (partial/storage_gap), a multi-revision window withholds it (invariant 43), low
coverage keeps the rate WITH fraction and reason. A gap also makes the single-revision
inference unsound, which the withholding covers by construction.

**P1-3 (one snapshot, one clock).** The whole report — watermark, segments, objective, burn,
repairing — is assembled inside ONE read-only REPEATABLE READ transaction
(`beginReportSnapshot`), whose first statement supplies `as_of` from the DB's
`statement_timestamp()`; burn staleness compares against that same instant. The application
clock appears nowhere. `ServiceHealthNow` runs in its own snapshot the same way.
`TestReportIsAssembledFromOneSnapshot` pins a snapshot, commits a concurrent invalidation
(watermark advance + new facts + new repair range), and proves the in-snapshot report is
entirely pre-mutation while a fresh report is entirely post-mutation. The `asOf` parameter
of `serviceReliabilityReportTx` is the explicit, testable clock seam.

**P2.** Diagnostics: a pending (never-confirmed) member now yields `unknown`, not a silent
pass; `failing_monitors` is sorted. Tenant scoping: `TestServiceReportSurfacesAreTenantScoped`
proves report/health/series return nothing and upsert mutates nothing under a real second
tenant's project (health gained an explicit ownership check — its epoch lookup is keyed by
service alone). Docs: the FR-021 status row and the phase-1 traceability row now read
"phase 2 IN_PROGRESS (iter-0138); phases 3+ unstarted".

**Round-2 mutants** (each compile+vet clean, killed on the discriminating assert, reverted):

- M12 repairing-read-escapes-the-snapshot (pool instead of tx) → "a post-snapshot repair
  range leaked into the snapshot report".
- M13 pending-reads-as-healthy → "diagnostics = ok over pending members, want unknown".
