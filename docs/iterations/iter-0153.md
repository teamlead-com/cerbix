## 1. What this arc makes true

**A frozen document is corrected in the next document, and no living document quotes a contract that
no longer exists.**

The first half is the contract's own rule and the reason it exists: an immutable report is evidence
of what was believed at a point in time, and evidence that gets rewritten is not evidence. The second
half is the part I keep failing at — three rounds in a row ([403], [405], [407]) found a sentence that
described a mechanism I had already replaced. Removing a mechanism is two acts, and I keep performing
one.

## 2. What round [409] found

### 2.1 The deleted scoreboard was still being quoted — five statements in three documents, one frozen

`cc247ce` deleted the cumulative "reviewer findings" scoreboard from `docs/status.md` and recorded
the reason in iter-0152 §2.10: a live self-referential total cannot converge, because every review of
the total is itself a finding that changes it. What `cc247ce` did NOT do is propagate that deletion
through the prose describing it. At the moment of freezing:

- `iter-0152.md:26` — "the CUMULATIVE counters live in `docs/status.md`";
- `iter-0152.md:54` — Agent C's output listed as "`docs/status.md` (the cumulative counters …)";
- `iter-0152.md:235-241` — "The cumulative counters now have exactly one owner, `docs/status.md`",
  followed by a paragraph analysing "the two totals" and how their SETS differ;
- `docs/status.md:28` — DoD-0151: "the cumulative totals are stated once, at the top of this
  document";
- `docs/traceability.md:203` — "the totals stated once at the top of `status.md`".

There is now exactly one counter — §2 finding SECTIONS in the frozen reports — and explicitly no
lifetime total. So all five sentences describe a document layout that no longer exists, and the first
three of them sit inside the very snapshot whose §2.10 announces the deletion.

The two living documents are corrected in this arc. The three frozen lines are NOT: see §3.

Why it happened, without dressing it up: I treated "delete the scoreboard" as a diff to one table
instead of a change to a contract. The contract was stated in five places because I had earlier been
told — correctly — to state where cumulative numbers live. Retiring that rule required retiring every
statement of it, and I checked the number instead of the rule.

### 2.2 A CLOSED snapshot was still the current, writable iteration

`docs/status.md:5` still read "Current iteration (iter-0152 …)" after `cc247ce` closed iter-0152 and
declared it immutable. Three consequences, all visible in the file:

- the living index pointed the next writer at a document that refuses writes;
- its round enumeration stopped at [403], while the frozen §2.10 of the report it was describing
  records [405] and [407] — the index was stale about its own subject;
- `423b2a6` is a commit made AFTER the closure, and the frozen report says anything later belongs in
  `iter-0153` — which did not exist, so the only writable home for it was the closed report's own
  header.

This is the same category as finding [401]: not an arithmetic error but an invalid place to put a
changing fact. Closing a report without opening its successor leaves the changing facts nowhere legal
to go, and they land back in the frozen one.

Two things change:

1. **This report is the current iteration.** `status.md` names iter-0152 as CLOSED at `cc247ce` and
   points here for the arc in progress.
2. **The living documents stop ENUMERATING review rounds.** `status.md:5` and
   `traceability.md:203` each carried a list of round IDs, and each had gone stale at a different
   round ([403] and [399] respectively) because a list in a living document grows with every round
   while nothing forces it to. Round IDs are per-arc evidence and they live in the report for that
   arc, which freezes. The living documents point at the reports instead. This is the same fix as
   deleting the scoreboard, applied to the other growing thing I had left in place — and it is why
   this arc's own round, [409], appears in THIS report and in no living index.

### 2.3 The report was opened; its live status items were not

Opening iter-0153 fixed where the writable iteration IS and left out what AGENTS' workflow step 2
actually asks for: `docs/status.md` updated with atomic `AC`/`DoD` items in `TODO`/`IN_PROGRESS`/`DONE`.
The file named iter-0153 as current and then went straight into the `AC-0151-*`/`DoD-0151` rows, all
still `DONE` — so the live checklist had nothing that was live. `rg 'AC-0153|DoD-0153'` returned
nothing.

That is the same failure as [409] P2-2 one level down, and worth naming as such rather than as a
missing table: I restored the POINTER to the writable iteration without restoring the thing the
pointer is for. A current-iteration header with no in-progress items is a heading, not a status.

Fixed by `AC-0153-1..3` and `DoD-0153`, all `IN_PROGRESS`, each linked to the section here that
defines it and to the §5 command that checks it; they flip to `DONE` only in the commit that closes
this report. The previous arc's rows keep their `DONE` state and move under their own
`## Previous iteration` heading, because they document iter-0151/iter-0152 and not this one.

### 2.4 This report miscounted its own sources, and misstated its own ordering

Two self-description defects in the document you are reading — [411] P2-1 and P2-2, one section
because they are the same class:

- it said the deleted contract survived in **four documents** — in the note above and in the §2.1
  heading — while §2.1's own list enumerates **five statements in three documents**: iter-0152, `docs/status.md`,

The pattern this arc keeps producing is not carelessness about facts but carelessness about the
SENTENCES ABOUT facts — a count restated beside its source, a claim added for reassurance. Both were
caught by a reader comparing two lines of mine, which is the cheapest possible check and one I can run
myself.

## 3. What cannot be fixed, and why that is the correct outcome

Three sentences inside iter-0152 are wrong and will stay wrong. Correcting them would mean editing a
report that its own text declares immutable from `cc247ce` — the exact defect [401] named, committed a
second time and with the freeze as the excuse. So the disposition is a record, not a repair:

> **Correction of record for iter-0152.** Where iter-0152 says cumulative counters live in
> `docs/status.md` (lines 26, 54 and 235–241), read its §2.10 instead, which is authoritative: the
> cumulative scoreboard was DELETED by `cc247ce`, not relocated. The only surviving counter is the
> count of §2 finding sections in the frozen reports, and `docs/status.md` states no lifetime total.

That is the price of immutability, and it is worth paying: a reader who runs
`git show cc247ce:docs/iterations/iter-0152.md` and a reader who opens the file today see the same
bytes, and both find the correction here, in a document dated after it. The alternative — a clean
snapshot with an untraceable edit history — is worse in exactly the way the rule was written to
prevent.
