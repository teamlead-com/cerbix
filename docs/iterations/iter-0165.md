### 1.4 The adversarial pass on the store, and five fixes (task 2, Agent B)

  hashed the string as given; PostgreSQL accepts an upper-case or brace-wrapped uuid everywhere else in the

  `TestChangeRecordAcceptsEveryUUIDSpellingAndAMalformedServiceIDIsNotFound`.

  universe is now rows with `recorded_at <= opened_at`; the anchor is each group's latest phase from that

### 1.10 The correlation stops depending on the notification (review [64], [70], [68])

The reviewer read the integration rather than the units and found what neither the store's tests nor the
worker's could see: `deliver`'s incident branch returned the moment `webhooks.Deliver` errored, and the
correlation call sat AFTER that return. A subscriber's endpoint failing — persistently, all the way to the
dead letter — therefore left a service auto-incident with no `incident_changes` links, no `🚀 Changes:`
note, and not even a counted error, because the attempt had never been made. D7 and NFR-020 fail open in
two directions and only one of them was implemented.

The delivery's error is now held while the correlation is attempted for the `opened` event, and only then
returned; the attempt runs on its own 15-second budget off `settleCtx`, so a delivery that spent its whole
budget cannot turn into a counted correlation error. Idempotence is what makes this safe under retries and
it already existed in the store: the transaction takes the incident row and returns `Skipped` when the
marker is already present, so a redelivery attempts again — it must, in case the first attempt failed —
and writes nothing. `attachIncidentContext` keeps its old position deliberately: the same shape is there
for FR-022's `⚡ Context:` note, and changing it is that requirement's decision to make, not this one's.

An existing test had pinned the defect — "a failed webhook must retry before any correlation" — so it is
corrected in place and says why, beside the new
`TestChangeCorrelationRunsThoughTheWebhookDeliveryFails`: one attempt though the delivery failed, the event
left undelivered and retryable, the retry attempting again while the store's `Skipped` keeps the metrics at
one, and a planted correlation error counted as the correlation's own while the webhook decides the event's
fate. Restoring the early return fails it. D7, invariant 8 and the discharge row now state both directions.

The first fix left one way out still above the correlation, and the reviewer found it at [70]: `Deliver`
erroring was handled, but no deliverer being CONFIGURED returned before the payload was even decoded, so
`webhooks: nil` — every worker built without a webhook registry — silently correlated nothing at all. It is
the same defect wearing the other hat, and the shape that removes both is to stop having a notification
error be a control-flow exit: every way the notification can fail now lands in one `derr`, the decode moves
above it, the correlation is attempted, and only then is `derr` returned.

[68] asked for the context claim of §1.10 to be pinned rather than asserted in prose, and
`TestChangeCorrelationSurvivesANilDelivererAndGetsItsOwnLiveBudget` now pins both findings at once: no
deliverer and the correlation still runs and counts (while a monitor incident with no deliverer still
correlates nothing — eligibility belongs to the incident, never to the notification), and a deliverer that
waits out the entire delivery budget still leaves the correlation a live context, bounded by its own 15 s
off `settleCtx`, cancelled when the attempt returns. Three mutations die on it: the nil-deliverer early
return, `linkPrecedingChanges(ctx, …)` on the spent delivery budget, and `settleCtx` bare and unbounded.

One test-harness defect surfaced on the way and is worth recording, because it produced a panic that looked
like a product bug: `newWorker(fs, nil, …)` takes a `*fakeWebhook` and hands it to `New`, so a nil pointer
became a NON-nil interface holding nil and `w.webhooks != nil` stayed true — the fake then panicked on its
own receiver. `newWorker` now maps a nil pointer to a nil interface, which is what "no deliverer" means, and
`TestUnknownTopicAndNilDeliverersFail` drops the hand-rolled conversion it had been carrying to work around
exactly this.

### 1.17 The pool was fenced by the wrong generation (review [41])

Third finding in a row against my own fix, and the reviewer's diagnosis was exact. `compareRows` stopped its
pool on `stale(mine)` — the PAGE's generation — while a horizon switch moves only the COMPARISONS' generation
(`cancelCompares()` is `cmpScope.begin()`). So a switch aborted the four in flight, scheduled a new pool for
every row, and left the old pool alive: its stop check saw an unchanged page generation, it kept dequeuing, and
each job read the CURRENT scope generation and was therefore not stale. Two pools of four ran at once and
