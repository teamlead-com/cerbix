## Source

1. **BUG-0202-1 — the reliability strip's cell readout is cut off.** Hovering a bucket near the left
   edge of a segment lane on the service page showed only the right half of its readout.
2. **BUG-0202-2 — the response-time readout is "very abrupt".** On the monitor page, hovering a
   recorded check made the readout flicker in and out and the card jump.
3. **BUG-0202-3 — the escalation-policy row "slid to the edge".** On the service page's paging
   ownership card, the `Escalation policy` row ran to the card's left edge. Reported by the owner
   after the review prompt for BUG-0202-1/-2 had been handed out, and added to this iteration.

## Root cause (read from the code, then reproduced in a browser)

1. `ReliabilityStrip.vue` placed the readout `absolute`, at the cell's centre with
   `translateX(-50%)` and nothing keeping it in bounds. The service card that holds every strip is
   `overflow-hidden` (`ServiceReliability.vue`, for its rounded corners), so any part of the readout
   outside the card was clipped: the left half at the left edge, the right half at the right edge,
   and the foot of a readout under the last lane.
2. `MonitorDetailView.vue`: the only pointer target of a check was its dot, `r = 2.6` units of a
   stretched viewBox — about 5–6 CSS pixels across on a ~1070 px plot — so the readout existed only while the pointer sat
   exactly on it. The readout was also inserted below the chart (`v-if`) only while something was
   hovered, so the card grew by a readout's height on every enter and shrank on every leave.
3. `ServiceAlerting.vue`: the escalation-policy row is deliberately outside the card body's
   container (a separate write with its own save and error), and the body's gutter (`px-4 py-3`) and
   type size (`text-[12.5px]`) were set only on that container. The row therefore had no left or
   bottom padding and fell back to the page's 14 px type.

## Changes (final, after re-review 1)

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `frontend/src/components/ReliabilityStrip.vue` | The readout is `position: fixed`, which no `overflow` ancestor clips. It is measured once rendered (hidden for that one frame) and placed in viewport coordinates: centred on its cell where it fits, pushed in from an edge where it does not (8 px margin), above the strip when there is no room below, and — R1-4 — clamped into the viewport when neither side holds it whole (it then overlaps its own pointer-transparent strip; one taller than the viewport is pinned to the viewport's top). A scroll or resize places it again. It stays in the strip's DOM, so its content is still the parent's slot. |
| `frontend/src/views/MonitorDetailView.vue` | **Pointer (R1-3):** one transparent layer over the plot hovers the NEAREST check within `POINT_HIT_PX` = 9 CSS px, measured on screen from the plot's live box (so the stretched viewBox and the plot's width cannot distort it); equal distances go to the earlier check; a failure with no latency is reached at its baseline mark's centre. Beyond the radius nothing is hovered. Keyboard focus stays on the dots. **Plot width (R1-1):** measured when the plot element APPEARS (a `watch` on its ref, `flush: "post"`), with a ResizeObserver, or on window resize where there is none; exposed as `data-plot-px`. **Slot (R1-2, R2-1, R2-2):** a one-cell grid holding what is always there — an invisible sizer shaped like the point readout and the widest-gap line — so its height is the taller of the two and depends on nothing hovered; the hovered readout (expectation cell, point, empty span) is laid over the slot out of flow (`absolute`, no z-index), so a taller one runs over what follows instead of pushing the card, and stays under the sticky top bar. While a readout is shown, the widest-gap line is hidden with `visibility` and keeps its place. |
| `frontend/src/components/ServiceAlerting.vue` | The escalation-policy row carries the body's gutter, bottom padding and type itself (`px-4 pb-3 text-[12.5px]`); its top border still spans the card. The body gets `data-testid="alerting-body"` for the layout test. |
| `e2e/tests/readouts.spec.ts` (new) | Eight browser tests: edges (desktop and 420 px), neither-side-fits, an open readout following its cell on scroll, hover near a dot, the nearest check, phone width (plot width, targets, baseline mark, long message, empty span), the sticky top bar, and the widest-gap line at 260 px. |
| `e2e/tests/service-layout.spec.ts` (new) | The escalation-row test. |
| `internal/web/dist` | SPA snapshot regenerated. |

**Not done, on purpose:** hovering anywhere in a column to reach the nearest check. In a long empty
interval that would show a check where cerbix observed nothing, and this panel states that it makes
no claim there. The nearest-check rule is bounded by the 9 px radius.

**Known limit:** a strip readout taller than the viewport is pinned to the viewport's top and its
foot stays off screen, unreadable there. Its height depends on the cell — how many sealed and
provisional states it carries, an unstored tail, the stored-buckets line — and on wrapping; the
reviewer built a valid day cell 234.5 px tall at 420 px wide. Nothing caps it.

overlaps by `pointer-events: none` elements and clipping inside its 2 px inset are outside it.

## Re-review 2 — APPROVED

No new findings in scope. The reviewer confirmed in a browser on the current stack: the top bar stays
above a readout scrolled under it and keeps its pointer events (a click into Search and typing, without
force); a tall readout stays over every conditional card below (credential source, conditions,
dependencies, open incident, configuration — none positioned, none a stacking context; a push monitor
has no response-time slot); dropping `z-[5]` rests on that paint order, to be revisited if positioned
content ever follows the slot. The card's height held at 260–1280 px for point, span and expectation
readouts, with the widest-gap line inside slot and card; Chromium's accessibility tree drops that line
while a readout shows (no manual screen-reader run). The documentation no longer overclaims. The
reviewer re-applied the two CSS mutations (readout `z-index: 10`; widest-gap `position: absolute`) in
their browser only — both killed by the shipped tests. Not re-run by the reviewer: full `make
dev-test`, build, snapshot regeneration (they are in the gates below).
